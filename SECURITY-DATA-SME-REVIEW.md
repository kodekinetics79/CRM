## Actual control architecture

**Tenant authority.** `server/platform.js:42–49,89–98` maps registry UUIDs to distinct SQLite workspaces and caches applications. Selection identifies a workspace; it does not authorize access. Separate tenant cookie names, independently checked tenant session hashes and the separate platform cookie prevent platform login from impersonating a tenant user. Missing nonlegacy storage fails rather than silently recreating an empty client database. Suspension revokes tenant sessions and stops scheduled generation through `isTenantActive`. This is application isolation within one process/filesystem/operator boundary, not isolated infrastructure or database row-level security.

**Sessions and mutation control.** Production requires HTTPS origin and secure, HttpOnly, Strict cookies; opaque session tokens are hashed, expire and time out idle. Current account role/active status is rechecked. Account access changes revoke sessions; the final active administrator is protected. CSRF, strict schemas, optimistic versions, synchronous transaction rollback and shared record guards protect business mutation. User/admin credentials are not returned by business snapshots. Loopback evaluator mode remains a distinct development-only, seeded database/cookie workflow; its HTTP exception is not a production HTTPS downgrade.

**Financial meanings and retained identity.** Ordinary income is the posted gift and its fully reconciled integer-cent allocations. Pledges, asks, matching claims, planned gifts and awards are separate commitments. Event payment linkage references compatible posted gifts; it does not collect payment. Grant receipt linkage requires recorded award/funder/accounting consistency; historical missing award fields are initialized without inventing an award. Fundraising/event/tribute/receipt shared-writer guards limit reclassification of linked source facts. Identity aliases preserve the source identity; survivor deletion and protected historical merges are denied. Migration mappings and retained document/tribute/receipt references require reconciliation rather than silently rewriting history.

**Proof is documentary.** `server/grantOperations.js:22` checks same-grant document linkage, current document version, immutable revision, kind/category, Final/Submitted status, evidence chronology and SHA-256 bytes before recording completion. History retains source grant/owner/document versions and saved privacy requirements; current document privacy also restricts reads. It records staff confirmation, not independently verified submission, funder acceptance or funding. Authorized minimal queue milestones contain recorded dates/owners only. Legacy reminders are suppressed only by an authorized exact grant/kind/date match; callback errors fail closed.

**Current and historical privacy.** `server/documents.js:32,40` filters revision metadata by its saved visibility and returns 404 for a private saved revision to nonadministrators even if the latest revision became Workspace-visible. Current private visibility also protects prior content. `server/tributes.js:38,41` requires current and saved donor-disclosure approval for viewer notification/history copies; revocation hides donor-containing originals from viewers while staff retain authorized originals. Grant history preserves saved private evidence requirements when current visibility becomes public. Curated report sources and schedules must retain these requirements rather than trust only the latest label.

**Human correspondence and receipts.** Deterministic templates/merge allowlists, selected recipients, current consent and full reference versions bind preparation/finalization. Merged or do-not-contact recipients cannot be used to bypass consent. Correspondence and tribute notifications remain **Not sent**. Numbered receipts log staff confirmation of actual Print and authorized hand-signing, preserve void/reissue chains and disclose sponsor benefits without calculating deductibility; noncash uses description without assigned tax value. There is no email, payment, physical printing/signing or legal certification performed by a model. Finalized tribute notifications currently lack a withdrawal/supersession release transition; preserving originals alone does not complete a buyer-approved correction workflow.

| Migration | `server/migration.js:20,31–34,168–169,226–248`: normalized JSON from mapped CSV, constituents/designations/posted gifts only, at most 500 rows across ten files; exact decimals to cents, staged/prior source IDs, preview digest tied to current dependencies, atomic commit and retained batch reconciliation. All installed workspace tables/BLOBs are included. Sessions/challenges/pending enrollment are cleared, immutable business/audit retained. | Schedule, monitor and retain funded off-host encrypted copies; approve RPO/RTO and time a restore with exact-cent and file-hash reconciliation. Platform registry, environment/provider keys and the separate MFA key are outside the archive. The operator supplies the tenant UUID: maintain a verified source-path→tenant catalogue. A future S3 store would require a corresponding object manifest/copy/restore process; current SQLite archive would not capture external bytes. |

Live SQLite/database directories are not explicitly provisioned with the archive's 0600/0700 restrictions (`server/app.js:70–71`, `server/platform.js:26–27`); their confidentiality depends on host account permissions and storage encryption. Production requires least-privilege service accounts, encrypted volume/database/log coverage and a verified secret store. `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEY` and `BACKUP_ENCRYPTION_KEY` must be distinct server-only keys, held/recoverable separately from datasets. Lost MFA custody can make restored enabled accounts unusable; backup-key loss makes archives unreadable. Rotation/re-encryption and personnel access evidence are not supplied by merely naming environment variables.

## Hosting, retention and cost decisions

Provider capabilities below were checked against current official sources; they describe options, not configuration or product certification. Prior repository notes about Render/Neon/Vercel are historical inspections, not fresh live evidence.

Render free web services have an ephemeral filesystem and cannot attach persistent disks. Paid services can use mounted persistent disks; persistence is confined to that mount. Thus a funded correctly mounted database path is essential for a SQLite option, with successful save/restart/restore proof. Provider disk snapshots are not an application-approved recovery drill. [Render free services](https://render.com/docs/free), [persistent disks](https://render.com/docs/disks).

Neon supports usage-priced PostgreSQL storage and configurable restore history; its pricing distinguishes database storage, compute, branch usage, history and transfer. Its restore window is a recovery feature, not ten-year financial-record retention. A project cannot change region in place; migration requires a new destination project and cutover. Select the approved region before importing real records and verify all branch/copy/telemetry locations. No low four-user subscription price can be promised without workload and retention measurements. [Neon pricing](https://neon.com/pricing), [region migration](https://neon.com/faqs/change-region-existing-neon-project).

S3 Object Lock operates on versioned objects; governance bypass authority differs from compliance retention, and legal holds are independent. Choose these controls only after the district approves disposition and hold authority—an indiscriminate immutable bucket can obstruct lawful disposal. Private access, restricted IAM and appropriate encryption/key custody need explicit configuration. [S3 Object Lock](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/object-lock.html), [security practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonS3/latest/userguide/security-best-practices.html).

The cost model must count original and revised files, historical snapshots, audit growth, database/WAL, backup frequency/retention, temporary snapshot disk and memory headroom, object versions/replicas, scanning, requests/retrieval, egress, KMS, monitoring and restore labor. S3 charges vary across storage class, requests, retrieval, transfer and replication; smaller archived objects and minimum durations can change costs. No numerical client storage allowance or funded service commitment is established here. [S3 pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/s3/pricing/).

## RFP/DPA and AI admission boundaries

Primary supplied evidence: final RFP pp.19/28–29 (C6–C8), Attachment E SDPC v2.2 pp.6,9–11,23–29, and September 10 Addendum 2. Public answers directly verified by the parent clarify Q54 reporting/schedules, Q56 privacy/retention and Q17 optional AI; they do not replace executed contractual approval. Q56's earlier broader geography answer and Addendum 2's later US/Canada stored-data restriction need final buyer clarification. Use **US/Canada for every stored copy** as the conservative design boundary; do not describe Addendum 2 as solely a staffing restriction. Approve host, database, object, backup, logs, AI and support-access arrangements and the full subprocessor inventory.

Names/contact data remain protected. The supplied retention minimum is ten years of history; DPA district-directed return/disposal and termination exceptions must be reconciled by privacy/legal, including applicable Utah obligations. The DPA describes provider confirmed-breach notice within 72 hours, assessment cooperation and Utah remedy procedures; a draft policy is not a staffed or exercised procedure. No independent pentest or proposal questionnaire is mandated by Q56, but buyer-accepted SOC 2/ISO 27001/equivalent evidence and any requested precontract Exhibit G remain evidence gates. Hosting attestations do not certify Wimblo/Kode. This review is neither a legal interpretation nor a signed DPA.

Actual optional model assistance uses Ollama boundaries (`server/ai.js:26–34,105–121`): restricted tenants cannot send records to the provider; only eligible staff/admin synthetic-policy tenants can request models. Rule priorities remain deterministic. The synthetic label is an immutable tenant policy designation, **not** content classification that prevents someone entering real personal data. Real-data admission must therefore be controlled operationally, and future real-data AI needs explicit approved purpose, fields, provider geography/retention/training terms, consent and key/budget controls. Human review, prompt instruction, bounded output and rejecting tool calls do not substitute for those approvals. Model tasks cannot write source records or execute business actions.

District policy prohibits attaching its payment account to CRM; current manual CSV/reconciliation and recorded payment-method fields preserve that boundary. Do not promise a live Stripe account connector, collect PAN/CVV/bank credentials in notes, or claim PCI compliance from absence of a payment form. Vendor/product experience and software-partner authorization remain separate mandatory eligibility evidence; no security control resolves that procurement gate.
